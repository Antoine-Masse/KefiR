	--> Categorical response (2 categories: non, oui). One question: do the proportions differ according to traitement? Counterpart of the chi-squared test.
2) Checks on the data.
	==> Counts per group and category (3 groups x 2 categories): temoin 12/18; froid 12/18; chaud 6/24 (non/oui).
	--> No approximation is made: the computation is exact, small counts do not make it wrong, only less decisive (unlike the chi-squared, which needs expected counts of 5).
3) Law of the data.
	==> Each group has its own proportions of the 2 categories (binomial law); "no difference": the same proportions in every group. There is no shape of the noise to compare.
4) Starting point (priors), set before seeing the data.
	==> 50 % chance that a difference exists (heads or tails: the method takes no side); in each group, every set of proportions equally likely in advance (uniform Dirichlet, Gunel and Dickey 1974).
	--> These values are not fitted on the data; step 5 varies them to see whether the answer holds.
5) Bayes factor and sensitivity to the priors. [contingencyTableBF() {BayesFactor}]

	==> Precision: closed-form computation, no draws; no simulation error.
	==> Sensitivity: concentration of the prior from 1 to 4, crossed with starting chances of a difference from 1 against 2 to 2 against 1. The leading answer then goes from 37.6 % to 81.2 %.
	--> Caution added: the answer can reverse with the starting point; it will be called not settled.

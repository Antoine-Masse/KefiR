1) Plan recognised: one factor, g, with 4 groups (a, b, c, d); N = 60.
	--> One question: is there a difference in y according to g? No preliminary test chooses the method: the shapes of the noise will be compared, not chosen.
2) Checks on the data.
	==> Sizes: 15 per group (4 groups, N = 60).
	==> Ties: none.
	==> Values far from their group: 1 of 60 beyond 1.5 interquartile ranges, 1 beyond 3. [Tukey, quantile() {stats}]
	--> No value is removed: the 4 shapes of the noise stay in the running, and the heavy-tailed ones are there for such values.
3) Starting point (priors), set before seeing the data.
	==> 50.0 % chance that a difference exists (even odds: the method takes no side); expected size r = 0.5 ("medium" scale of Rouder et al. 2012); each of the 4 shapes of the noise starts at 25.0 %.
	==> With more than 2 groups, two forms of a difference start equal: all groups may differ, or one group alone stands apart.
	--> These values are not tuned on the data; step 5 varies them to see whether the answer holds.
4) Comparison of the shapes of the noise (how values scatter around the centre of their group: normal or heavy tails, equal variances or not). [m.bay() {KefiR}, importance sampling]
	==> Posterior probabilities: normal, equal variances 67.2 %; heavy tails, equal variances 23.1 %; normal, unequal variances 6.7 %; heavy tails, unequal variances 3.0 %.
	==> Equal variances probable (90.3 %).
	--> The normal shapes are the more probable (73.9 %): the answer bears mainly on the means.
	--> No shape is chosen: the answer of each one is weighted by its probability.
5) Bayes factor and numerical precision. [m.bay() {KefiR}]
	==> Bayes factor, difference against none: 623 (the data favour a difference).
	==> Imprecision of the computation by simulation: about 0.66 % on the Bayes factor, without effect on the conclusion (this is not a risk of error).
	==> Sensitivity: expected size r from 0.25 to 1, starting chances of a difference from 1 against 2 to 2 against 1, spread of the variances between groups. "y differs according to g" then goes from 99.5 % to > 99.9 %. [m.bay() {KefiR}]
	--> The answer does not depend on these choices.
6) Which groups differ, and by how much. [lmBF(), posterior() {BayesFactor}, hdi() {bayestestR}]
	==> Most probable groupings (15 compared): a = c < b = d (58.6 %); a = c < b < d (21.4 %); a < c < b = d (14.3 %).

	Estimated gap: posterior median, pulled slightly towards zero by the prior, hence a little smaller than the observed gap of step 7. Brackets: 95 % credible interval, if a difference exists (the value lies there with 95 % probability; not a confidence interval).
	--> All pairs come from one single law over the groupings: no correction pair by pair is needed, and the answers cannot contradict each other.

	Verdict: a difference very probably exists. This says neither that it matters nor why; the pairs are below.
	Probability of each hypothesis, given the data and the starting point:
